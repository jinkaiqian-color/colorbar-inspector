How the bar is found

  1. Every filled rectangle is collected with its size, position and ink. Images, text, curved shapes and shapes in production inks (dieline, registration, cut) are set aside.
  2. Rectangles of the same size are grouped. A color bar is a group on one straight line, advancing by a constant pitch, with at least three different fills.
  3. The best-scoring line wins. Smaller rectangles inside its outline, such as a tint ladder stacked in one cell, are attached as sub-patches.
  4. If a QR-style corner mark (three concentric squares in the ratio 7 : 5 : 3) sits in line with the bar, just past one end, the patch next to the mark becomes the first patch and the bar is read away from it. Without a mark, the bar is read left to right, or top to bottom.

Limits

The bar has to be live vector artwork. A bar placed as an image, or converted to outlines, cannot be read. Spot colors are labeled S1, S2 and so on in order of appearance, and their preview colors are approximate. Rotated pages are reported in unrotated page coordinates.
